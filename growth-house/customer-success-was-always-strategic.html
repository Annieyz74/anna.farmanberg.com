Customer Success Was Always Strategic — Now It’s Undeniable

13 August 2026

What happens to Customer Success when product adoption no longer tells us whether customers are actually creating value?

Adoption still matters, but the outcome we should care most about is value. And value is always defined in the customer’s context. Two customers can use the same product in the same way and still describe value completely differently. It shifts depending on their priorities, constraints and the problems they’re trying to solve.

That’s why Customer Success needs to understand three different signals: activity, perceived value and realized value, and more importantly, the gaps between them.

Contract size, product adoption and even renewal don’t necessarily tell the whole value story. Customers can feel they’re getting value in ways that never show up in a vendor’s metrics. At the same time, high usage doesn’t guarantee a meaningful business outcome is actually being achieved. AI makes this contrast sharper, because it changes workflows, decisions and processes. And that’s exactly why CS needs a different kind of value conversation. This shift forces organizations to rethink how work happens, and that’s a very different challenge from simply changing how tools are used.

This shifts the Customer Success question from: “Are they using it?” to: “Has anything meaningful changed?”

I believe this is a good thing but it’s a different conversation, because it opens the door for Customer Success to play a more strategic role.

Organizations are investing heavily in AI capability right now. CS sits close to the customer’s reality and sees the habits, constraints and operational details that determine whether AI capability turns into actual outcomes. But capability is only one part of the equation, the rest comes from the structures and behaviors that turn it into value. In a moving customer context, the real leverage comes from how well CS can work with shifting priorities, constraints and stakeholders to turn capability into outcomes.

This is where I see Customer Success becoming a link between AI capability and customer value: understanding how technology becomes valuable in the customer’s context and helping customers integrate it into the workflows where value is actually created.

As AI accelerates, understanding context becomes a real asset. And Customer Success is one of the functions built to work in that context.
