What Happens When AI Capability Is No Longer the Bottleneck?

20 August 2026

What happens when AI capability is no longer the bottleneck?

We’ve seen AI models become more capable and accessible than ever this year.

Now we see organizations moving from pilots into production, and AI is finding its way into more workflows, functions and everyday tasks. I think it’s interesting to see that organizations end up with very different outcomes even though they use similar technology.

This signals that having access to AI and having the capability to use it are two very different things, and so are usage and change. The same goes for implementation, which alone does not guarantee value.

So, what makes an organization AI-capable?

I believe it’s about choosing the right problems to solve, understanding the purpose behind the change so people know what they’re moving toward, redesigning work instead of digitizing old workflows, and connecting new technology with existing systems and data that actually support change.

Organizations also have to create environments where people can learn, experiment and change their behavior. One of the most important abilities leaders need now is to hold steady when things get uncomfortable, because they will.

In my last post, I wrote about shifting the Customer Success question from “Are they using it?” to “Has anything meaningful changed?” Organizations should probably ask themselves the same question.

AI can accelerate change, but it cannot create organizational readiness for us. Maybe the next advantage will belong to organizations that are better at absorbing change and turning it into value.
