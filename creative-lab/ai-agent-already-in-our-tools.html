How Much of Tomorrow’s AI Agent Is Already in the Tools We Use Today?

11 November 2026

How much of tomorrow’s AI agent is already in the tools we use today?

I’ve noticed AI agents coming up more often in everyday conversations lately. I was talking to someone the other day who told me their company was starting to explore building its own AI agents.

AI has become a natural topic of conversation, which I think is a good thing. But the more I hear the term “AI agents”, the more I wonder what people mean by it. It also makes me curious about how much of what they want to achieve is possible with the tools they already use.

Take email, for example. Many of us manage both our own inbox and shared inboxes with colleagues. Let’s say we want to work more efficiently with those inboxes. Before choosing a solution, we first need to understand what that means in practice:

  • How should incoming emails be assigned?
  • Who takes responsibility?
  • How do colleagues know what has already been handled?

Once we understand the needs and agree on a way of working we can explore the options:

  • How far could existing features, such as Outlook rules and categories, take us?
  • Where might a workflow in MS Power Automate help?
  • What additional value would an AI agent bring?

This is familiar ground from a Customer Success perspective: understand the desired outcome, the people and the process, then work out what needs to change.

And as AI capabilities develop, we need to revisit and perhaps challenge our assumptions about what our tools can do. Something we tested a few months ago may be worth another look today. As AI assistants become more capable, the additional business value of building our own agents needs to become clearer.

Perhaps building our own AI agent turns out to be step three.

  • First, understand what we need to achieve
  • Then, explore how far our existing tools can take us
  • Build where there is a clear need for something more
