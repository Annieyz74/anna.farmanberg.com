What Becomes Better for the Customer? And How Do We Know?

8 October 2026

What becomes better for the customer? And how do we know?

When was the last time you asked yourself, a colleague or a team these two questions? I’m sure you’re familiar with goals, targets, priorities and budget. Also, product roadmaps, value propositions, sales targets and more.

As most of us know, change is the only constant, and everyone is doing their best to keep up.

Have you ever been to a meeting with the goal to present X while the customer says, “Well, that’s interesting but we’d rather solve Y”. I have.

It is easy for an inside-out perspective to creep in, even when the intention is to be customer-centric. That’s why I believe a couple of questions can help us stay focused, even when things move fast.

What becomes better for the customer? This question brings us back to the outcome and value we want to create.

How do we know? This asks for evidence that the value was actually realized.

These questions are a great way to stay on track in our daily work. To check whether we are still moving towards something customers actually value. Because if we can’t tell what’s improving for the customer and how to measure realized value, then we should probably rethink.

Two simple questions, but surprisingly easy to lose sight of.
